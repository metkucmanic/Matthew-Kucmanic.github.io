About me
======
Welcome to my page! I am a fourth year geography PhD candidate in the [School of Earth, Environment and Sustainability](https://sees.uiowa.edu/) at the University of Iowa. 

My current research examines the friction points between genomic research and the neurodiversity movement, especially how different assemblages of environment are used by researchers and neurodivergent people (specifically ADHDers). Overall, I also aim to develop ethical approaches for genomic research that brings greater awareness and justice to ADHD and neurodivergent people. 


Why Human Geography?
======
Human geography is not just about maps, shapes, and boundaries. Critical human geography challenges many of these cartographies and thinks about the interactions, ecologies, and people that live and produce space. As such, human geography is uniquely relational and thinks about the how states, objects, and bodies exist as a nexus of multiple forces. In terms of health, human geography is focused on the material production of health where biological body is not separated from the ecologies that it inhabits. Likewise, disability (in addition to health) is both an embodied and emergent practice where the biology of the body and the environment that is seemingly outside the body produce an interactive 'body-world' where our genes, neurotransmitters, neurons, brains, bones, muscles, etc. that make up our bodies are in constant relation to the air, heat, moisture, toxins, food, other bodies, computers, apartments, homes, etc. that make up our environments. As such, human geography allows for a rich and critical understanding of disability and health that transcends disciplinary and material categorizations. 

Background
======
Before coming to the University of Iowa, I worked at Case Western Reserve University (CWRU) as a public health specialist for 5 years. There, I was involved in several projects that addressed health disparities in Cleveland, Ohio and led me to explore the spatial aspects of these disparities. During this time, I also began to to become critical about how we were mapping bodies onto space and how spatial epidemiology/mapping had become a 'hot' new way for implementing a more 'precise' public health. 

My work as a public health specialist, grew out of my graduate work at CWRU. At CWRU, I received a Masters in Public Health and a Masters of Arts in Bioethics with my capstone project involving interviews with Public Health researchers and practitioners about their thoughts of the precision medicine initiative. 

Personally, I grew up in Cleveland, Ohio and come from a large Croatian family. In undergraduate, I received scholarship to perform the songs and dances of Eastern Europe as a member of the Duquesne University Tamburitzans, where we performed ~75 shows per year. While in Cleveland, I was a member of the Cleveland Orchestra Chorus and now continue to sing in Vocal Artists of Iowa. 

What motivated me to pursue my current research is the growing acknowledgement of the neurodiversity movement and my own identity as a queer ADHD person. What struck me the most about the movement was how biological definitions were acknowledged by various scholars and people on social media, but pathological notions were strongly rejected. I was also struck by how conversations seemed to acknowledge how shifting environments changed everyday symptoms/performances of ADHD and other neurodivergent traits and that ethical frameworks appeared to be developing around these shifts in environments.
